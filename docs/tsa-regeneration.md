# Tutorial: TSA regeneration (illustrative)

> **Illustrative deck, not a validated reproduction.** No temperature swing experiment is bundled with Skarstrom yet, so this tutorial uses a deck built for the documentation. The adsorbent is a cited [library entry](adsorbent-library.md#zeolite-13x-perez2019) and the column is that of the Perez (2019) experiments, but the operating conditions (feed velocity, step times, medium temperatures and heat transfer coefficients) are round numbers we chose, and the heating step takes the isotherm above its 298–348 K fit range. Use it to learn the workflow, not as design data.

**Goal.** Capture CO₂ from a 15 % CO₂ / 85 % N₂ flue gas with a four-step, indirectly heated temperature swing adsorption (TSA) cycle: adsorb cold, heat the bed to release CO₂, cool it down again, re-pressurise. You will heat and cool the column with a per-step **heating medium**: steam or hot oil at 150 °C, then cooling water at 25 °C, both flowing through a jacket on the wall and an internal heat exchanger in the packing. You will run to cyclic steady state, read purity, recovery and the thermal energy and cooling duty per kilogram of CO₂, and see why a 100 °C jacket alone would not do on this column.

---

## 1. Load the deck

```python
import skarstrom

sim = skarstrom.examples.Illustrative_TSA_13X.load()
print(sim.steps.names)  # ['Adsorption', 'Heating', 'Cooling', 'Pressurisation']
```

The deck is generated by the `IllustrativeTSA13X` test in `core/test/integration/test_tsa_example.cpp`, which builds it in code (the clearest example of setting up a heating medium in C++), re-saves it to `examples/decks/` and checks that it still runs. `examples/tsa_illustrative.py` builds the same deck in Python, runs it and plots the bed temperatures.

## 2. Walk through the setup

**Fluid.** *CarbonDioxide* and *Nitrogen*.

**Adsorbents.** One material, *Zeolite 13X (Perez 2019)*, added unchanged from the curated library (`AddLibraryAdsorbent` in C++; `skarstrom.adsorbent_path("zeolite_13x_perez2019")` gives the file). It carries the competitive dual-site Langmuir isotherm (concentration form) from Table 2 of Estupiñan Perez et al. (2019), a macropore-controlled linear driving force with tortuosity 3, and heats of adsorption of 30.85 kJ/mol (CO₂) and 15.93 kJ/mol (N₂). The library entry states the isotherm was fitted at 298–348 K and 0–1 bar.

**Column.**

- *Geometry*: a cylinder of 0.032 m diameter, ambient temperature 298 K. One layer, *13X*: 0.30 m long, 30 cells (with the default WENO advection the recovery is grid-converged on about 20), bed voidage 0.41, particle diameter 1.5 mm, momentum model *Ergun* (the default for a new layer; the Perez2019 decks use *Darcy (Kozeny–Carman)*, see [What to try next](#5-what-to-try-next)).
- *Energy Balance*: non-isothermal, local thermal equilibrium, adsorbed-phase heat capacity 30.7 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. The layer uses constant internal (bed-to-wall) and external (wall-to-ambient) heat transfer coefficients of 8.6 and 10 W m⁻² K⁻¹ and an axial thermal conductivity of 0.0903 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹, as in the Perez2019 decks.
- *Wall*: material *SS316L* (density, heat capacity and conductivity come from the [wall material library](wall-energy-balance.md#wall-materials)), 3.1 mm thick. That is about 0.8 kg of steel, with a heat capacity of about 400 J/K, more than the 0.16 kg of zeolite it holds.

**Cycle.** Four steps, 2430 s per cycle:

| Step | Time | Bottom (feed end) | Top | Heating medium |
|------|------|-------------------|-----|----------------|
| Adsorption | 600 s | **Flow** inlet *Flue gas*: 0.05 m/s superficial, 15 % CO₂, 298 K | **Pressure** outlet, 1 bar | none (ambient) |
| Heating | 900 s | **Pressure** outlet, 1 bar: CO₂ product | **Closed** | 150 °C |
| Cooling | 900 s | **Closed** | **Closed** | 25 °C |
| Pressurisation | 30 s | **Pressure** inlet, linear ramp to 1 bar, feed gas at 298 K | **Closed** | none (ambient) |

The *Heating* and *Cooling* steps each have a [heating medium](wall-energy-balance.md#heating-medium-jacket). It is a fluid at a set temperature that reaches the column in two ways:

- **Jacket**, on the wall's outer face, with $h_m$ = 500 W m⁻² K⁻¹. This is a typical figure for condensing steam or a pumped liquid in a jacket. While the step runs it replaces the ambient exchange. The heat still has to cross the wall and then the 8.6 W m⁻² K⁻¹ bed-to-wall coefficient, which is what limits it.
- **Internal heat exchanger**, tubes in the packing, with $a_x$ = 50 m² of surface per m³ of bed and $h_x$ = 40 W m⁻² K⁻¹. 50 m²/m³ is about one 10 mm tube per 8 cm² of cross-section. 40 W m⁻² K⁻¹ is within the 20–100 W m⁻² K⁻¹ usual for a gas-filled packed bed on an immersed surface. Together, $a_x h_x$ = 2000 W m⁻³ K⁻¹ heats the bed with a time constant of about five minutes. The model counts the tubes' heat transfer but not the space they take up.

In Python these are the step's `medium` fields: `sim.steps["Heating"].medium.temperature`, `.heat_transfer_coefficient`, `.exchanger_area_per_volume` and `.exchanger_heat_transfer_coefficient`. Their parameter paths are `step[Heating].medium.*`.

A medium is not a fixed duty. Its heat flow is proportional to how far the column is from the medium temperature, so the bed approaches 150 °C and never passes it. An [energy source](energy-balance.md#energy-source-heating-cooling) is different: it adds a fixed power for as long as the step lasts, whatever the bed temperature.

Heating vents the bed counter-currently, through the feed end, so the CO₂ comes out where it is most concentrated and the top of the bed stays clean for the next adsorption step. Cooling with both ends closed lets the pressure fall as the gas cools and CO₂ readsorbs; the pressurisation step then refills the bed with feed.

**Cyclic steady state.** *Mass Balance (Per-Component)*, tolerance 0.005, minimum 5 cycles, at most 60 cycles.

**KPI configuration.** CO₂ is tracked. **Feed**: the *Flue gas* stream of Adsorption and the pressure inlet of Pressurisation. **Product**: the Heating pressure outlet.

## 3. Run

```python
result = sim.run()
print(result.status, result.css_cycle + 1, f"{result.wall_time_s:.1f} s")
# reached_css 27 4.9 s
```

CSS is reached at cycle 27, with no step retries, in about 5 s natively.

## 4. Interpret the results

**KPIs.** At cycle 26 (the twenty-seventh cycle, counted from 0):

```python
k = next(k for k in sim.kpis(cycle=-1) if k.component == "CarbonDioxide")
```

| KPI | Value |
|---|---|
| CO₂ purity | 86.31 % |
| CO₂ recovery | 95.97 % |
| Production rate | 6.05 × 10⁻⁵ mol/s |
| Productivity (mass) | 1.64 × 10⁻⁵ kg CO₂ per kg adsorbent per second |
| Electrical energy | 0 |
| Thermal energy | 10.87 MJ per kg CO₂ |
| Cooling duty | 10.73 MJ per kg CO₂ |

```python
print(k.electrical_energy, k.thermal_energy, k.cooling_duty)    # J/kg
print(k.step_thermal_energy, k.step_cooling_duty)                # each step's share
```

The **electrical energy** counts compressor and vacuum-pump work, so it is zero here.

The **thermal energy** is the heat the medium put into the column, the logged [medium duty](wall-energy-balance.md#medium-duty). Over the Heating step that is 70.3 kJ per cycle. The CO₂ produced is 6.05 × 10⁻⁵ mol/s × 2430 s = 0.147 mol (6.47 g), which gives **10.87 MJ per kg of CO₂** (10.87 GJ/t; see [energy units](performance-metrics.md#energy-the-three-figures-and-their-units)).

The **cooling duty**, 69.4 kJ per cycle or 10.73 MJ/kg, is the heat the cooling water took out during Cooling. It is reported separately, not subtracted from the thermal energy; in a real plant it would come on top of it. `step_thermal_energy` puts all the heat on *Heating* and `step_cooling_duty` all the cooling on *Cooling*.

Of the 70.3 kJ, only 13.9 kJ enters through the internal exchanger; the other 56.4 kJ comes through the jacket. Most of the jacket's heat warms the steel wall from about 300 K to 423 K (400 J/K × 123 K ≈ 49 kJ), and the cooling water takes it out again in the next step. On a small laboratory column, cycling the wall's temperature costs more energy than the bed itself. In C++ the split is on each step's log (`StepHeatDuty::exchanger_heat_in`; see [medium duty](wall-energy-balance.md#medium-duty)).

**Why 150 °C, and why the exchanger.** We first tried the medium at 100 °C. With 13X at 1 bar the bed has to get well above 373 K to give up its CO₂. Through a jacket the bed can never pass the medium temperature, and the 8.6 W m⁻² K⁻¹ bed-to-wall coefficient slows its approach. The same cycle with other media (25 °C cooling water in every row; Ergun, last cycle):

| Heating medium | Purity | Recovery | Thermal energy | Hottest bed temperature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 °C, jacket only ($h_m$ = 500) | 52 % | 13 % | 44 MJ/kg | 354 K |
| 100 °C, jacket + exchanger | 76 % | 44 % | 14.7 MJ/kg | 370 K |
| 150 °C, jacket only | 79 % | 51 % | 18.8 MJ/kg | 388 K |
| **150 °C, jacket + exchanger (deck)** | **86 %** | **96 %** | **10.9 MJ/kg** | **421 K** |

A 100 °C jacket alone mostly heats and cools the wall. The bed only reaches 354 K, recovers little CO₂ and costs 44 MJ per kilogram. Raising $h_m$ does not help, because the bed-to-wall coefficient limits the heat flow, not the jacket. The internal exchanger heats the bed directly, and the hotter medium lets the bed reach 421 K, where 13X releases most of its CO₂.

**Temperatures.** The axial temperature profile at the end of each step of the last cycle:

```python
for i, name in enumerate(sim.steps.names):
    T = sim.profile("temperature", cycle=-1, step=i)
    print(f"{name:15s} {T.values[-1].min():6.1f} - {T.values[-1].max():6.1f} K")
```

- at the end of **Adsorption** the bed runs from 299 K at the feed end to 327 K at the top: the heat of adsorption travels ahead of the CO₂ front;
- at the end of **Heating** it is at 413–421 K, just short of the medium's 423 K, and the wall is at 423 K;
- at the end of **Cooling** it is back to 300 K, close to the cooling water's 298 K.

`sim.series("temperature", z=0.15)` gives the temperature at one position over the last cycle; with `cycle=0`, `1`, … you can watch it settle into the same loop every cycle.

**Product quality.** The CO₂ mole fraction leaving the bottom during *Heating* (step index 1):

```python
y = sim.stream_series(1, "Heating Pressure Boundary Bottom", "mole_fraction", "CarbonDioxide")
F = sim.stream_series(1, "Heating Pressure Boundary Bottom", "molar_flow_rate")
```

The first gas out of the bed is the feed left in the voids, 15 % CO₂. The outlet then enriches as the bed heats: 77 % CO₂ after 150 s, 96 % after 300 s, 98 % after 450 s and 99 % from 600 s to the end of the step. The diluted gas from the start of the step is why the overall purity is 86 % even though most of the CO₂ leaves at above 95 %. The molar flow on the same stream grows as desorption speeds up.

## 5. What to try next

- **Change the medium.** The medium's temperature and coefficients are registry parameters: `sim.steps["Heating"].medium.temperature = u.Quantity(120, "degC")` in Python, or `--set "step[Heating].medium.temperature=393.15"` on the command line. The thermal energy KPI follows. Setting `step[Heating].medium.exchanger_area_per_volume` to 0 gives the jacket-only rows of the table above. Setting the jacket coefficient `step[Heating].medium.heat_transfer_coefficient` to 0 leaves the exchanger alone: the wall then no longer swings through 125 K each cycle.
- **Use an electric heater instead.** Disable the medium and give the step an [energy source](energy-balance.md#energy-source-heating-cooling) on the *13X* layer, for example `sim.steps["Heating"].add_energy_source("13X", power=30)`, with a negative power on *Cooling*. A source puts in a fixed power whatever the bed temperature, so the bed keeps heating for as long as the step lasts. An earlier version of this deck used +30 W and −12 W: 84 % purity, 67 % recovery and 6.1 MJ/kg. A fixed duty puts its heat straight into the bed and does not swing the wall's temperature, so it costs less heat per kilogram, but it has no upper temperature limit.
- **Discard the diluted start of the heating step.** Split *Heating* into two steps, say 150 s and 750 s, each with the 150 °C medium, and mark only the second step's outlet as Product in the KPI configuration so the first is vented. Purity should rise and recovery fall. Splitting steps is a deck edit (see the C++ test that builds this deck); from Python you can estimate the effect from the stream logs, integrating the Heating outlet flows from 150 s onwards.
- **Heat with a hot purge instead.** Disable the Heating medium and give the step a top **Flow** inlet of N₂ whose **Temperature Profile** is *Linear* from 298 K to 423 K (see [inlet temperature profiles](boundary-conditions.md#inlet-temperature-profile)). The purge sweeps CO₂ out faster but dilutes the product.
- **Add a vacuum.** Lower the Heating outlet pressure (a temperature–vacuum swing; `step[Heating].bottom.pressure`) and give that boundary a vacuum pump with power calculation so the electrical energy KPI picks up the pump work.
- **Use a direct air capture sorbent.** The library's [APDES-NFC](adsorbent-library.md#apdes-nfc-stampi2020) amine sorbent has a temperature-dependent Toth isotherm for CO₂ from Stampi-Bombelli et al. (2020). Its rate constant is 2 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹, so adsorption steps last hours rather than minutes.
- **Compare the momentum models.** Switch the layer's momentum model (`layer[13X].momentum_model`) to *Darcy (Kozeny–Carman)*, as in the Perez2019 decks. At 0.05 m/s through 1.5 mm particles the inertial (Burke–Plummer) term of Ergun is small, so the results barely move: in our native run the cycle again reached CSS at cycle 27, with a CO₂ purity of 86.31 % and a recovery of 95.97 %. Both models cross this cycle's abrupt step switches without a solver retry.
- **Sweep the heating time** (`step[Heating].time`) in a [parametric study](parameterisation.md) and plot recovery against the thermal energy (`k.thermal_energy`).
