### Fixed — molecular diffusivity of a pure component with three or more components
- In a cell of pure component i, in a fluid of three or more components, Blanc's law is 0 / 0, and the engine gave D_m,i = 0. Every diffusion-based mass-transfer coefficient of i (macropore, film) was then 0, so i did not adsorb in that cell, for example in a bed that starts filled with pure N2. Blanc's law now uses a smooth floor on the partner mole fractions, ½ (y_j + √(y_j² + δ²)) with δ = 1e-6. In a pure cell, D_m,i is the harmonic mean of the D_ij. D_m,i always lies between the smallest and the largest D_ij, and it is a smooth function of the mole fractions, which the solver's difference-quotient Jacobian resolves. Where every partner has y_j ≥ 1e-3, the value equals Blanc's law to 2e-7.
- The molecular diffusivities are tested against Cantera with the engine's Lennard-Jones parameters (`scripts/gen_cantera_diffusion_reference.py`): the binary D_ij of all 528 component pairs agree to 0.31 % from 200 K to 650 K, and the mixture-averaged values to 0.2 %.
